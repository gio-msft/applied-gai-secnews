 The most robust model still fails frequently on falsification, showing that “lying in-spec” is a more reliable attack than obvious instruction overrides for compliance artifact generation.
 - Across 3,000 prompt-injection trials targeting six industrial compliance-artifact generation tasks, 65.1% succeeded, indicating that LLM-produced SDS/QC/DPP-style outputs are broadly susceptible to instruction manipulation at inference time.
 - Falsification payloads dominated every scenario (74–97% success by task) and remained highly effective even on the strongest model tested (45% success on Claude Sonnet), implying that pipelines relying on single-source LLM transformations are especially vulnerable to silent misinformation rather than overt jailbreak-style overrides.
 - Model choice and injection placement were decisive: open-weight models were far more vulnerable (Qwen2.5-7B 89.7%, Llama-3.1-8B 85.5%, Mistral-7B 83.8%) than frontier APIs (GPT-4o 55.8%, Claude Sonnet 10.7%), and active prompt-appended injections outperformed passive document-embedded injections (73.2% vs 57.0%), underscoring the need for strong prompt-boundary controls plus output verification and cross-checking.

🛡️ **RAVEN: A Self-RAG Based Agent for Threat Classification and Response** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25832v1.pdf) #cyber `6/10` 

 *Pironti Francesco Aurelio, Arena Luigi, Blefari Francesco et al. (DIMES - University of Calabria, IMT School for Advanced Studies)*

 Self-RAG-style relevance filtering offers its clearest value on hard-to-spot, context-sensitive web attacks (SSTI) while keeping remediation safer via an explicit analyst checkpoint and infrastructure grounding.
 - A training-free Self-RAG triage pipeline improved binary malicious/benign classification most on context-dependent SSTI payloads, raising F1 from 0.84 (LLM-only) to 0.92 (Self-RAG), while gains were marginal on well-patterned SQLi/XSS where F1 was already ~0.99.
 - Across three balanced 400-sample test sets, retrieval boosted SQLi performance from F1 0.96 (LLM-only) to 0.99 (Self-RAG) and reduced false positives, but provided no meaningful lift for XSS where all variants tied at F1 0.99, implying retrieval/reflection primarily helps where signatures are ambiguous rather than obvious.
 - An analyst-gated, two-stage design prevents unreviewed automated remediation and produces infrastructure-grounded artifacts (perimeter firewall rule plus host/port-specific hardening) by conditioning on a lightweight YAML environment model, with the key operational risk shifting to the completeness and freshness of that infrastructure description.

🛰️ **ALIoTh: Orchestrating Multi-Agent LLMs for Autonomous IoT Penetration Testing** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25809v1.pdf) #cyber `6/10` 

 *Pironti Francesco Aurelio, Arena Luigi, Blefari Francesco et al. (University of Calabria)*

 An IoT-specific, multi-agent LLM pentesting pipeline that couples Self-RAG grounding with planner/executor separation and safety kill-switches, aiming to make multi-device attack chains run end-to-end with measurable time-to-compromise metrics.
 - ALIoTh operationalizes autonomous IoT/IIoT penetration testing via a workflow-driven, multi-agent design that separates strategic planning (planner) from tool-grounded execution (phase-specific executors) and coordinates them through a shared blackboard for iterative replanning across multiple devices.
 - Self-RAG is used as a built-in control mechanism to reduce command-level hallucinations by forcing agents to retrieve and self-validate tool syntax, protocol knowledge (e.g., RTSP/MQTT/CoAP/BLE), and vulnerability intelligence before acting, with an explicit Human-in-the-Loop escalation path for ambiguous or high-impact steps.
 - The evaluation is defined around operationally meaningful metrics—Time to RCE, Time to Shell, Time to Root, total token consumption, and Success Rate—on a smart-home testbed of three known-vulnerable targets (IP camera default creds/RTSP hijack, smart plug header-based command injection, MQTT gateway anonymous subscribe), enabling direct measurement of end-to-end attack automation efficiency and reliability.

📧 **JEV vs. LLMs: A Comparative Analysis for Phishing Email Detection** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23683v1.pdf) #cyber `6/10` 

 *Muhammad Mahir Malik, Muhammad Moiz Khalid, Hussain Ahmad et al. (University of Central Punjab, National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences, Adelaide University, Zayed University)*

 A typed, decision-only model can outperform and undercut general-purpose LLMs on phishing email classification when you measure not just accuracy, but also latency, cost, and calibration.
 - On a 1,500-email binary dataset, Jev 1.13 achieved the top detection performance (79.67% accuracy, 97.97% recall, 82.62% F1), implying fewer missed phishing emails than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (76.20% accuracy) and Llama 4 Maverick (72.84%) while still leaving notable false-positive load due to 71.43% precision.
 - Jev 1.13 was operationally cheaper and faster for high-volume triage, finishing the full batch in 173.88s with 597,895 tokens and costing $0.015103 per 1,000 decisions—about 34% lower latency and ~80% lower cost than the average of the three LLM baselines (263.94s and $0.075565 per 1,000).
 - Reported confidence was most reliable for Jev 1.13 (Brier 0.1698, ECE 0.1594), with Nemotron 3 Ultra close behind (Brier 0.1762, ECE 0.1618), suggesting structured decision interfaces can better support confidence-threshold routing in phishing pipelines than free-form LLM self-reported confidence.
 - Note: The evaluation uses one Kaggle dataset and a single shared prompt per LLM, so rankings may shift under repeated trials, prompt tuning, or full-email content inputs.

🛂 **Protecting the System, Stranding the User: The Limits of Multi-Agent Authorization** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19598v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Gurpriya Bhatia, Clifford Neuman (University of Southern California)*

 Multi-agent authorization can “win” on integrity while still making the human lose, and fixing that requires designing refusal, handoff, and recovery around user outcomes—not just stopping bad tool calls.
 - Because human intent has no formal, observable ground truth, labeling semantic alignment as “verified intent” is a category error and authorization should instead expose an explicitly probabilistic intent-fidelity estimate whose required threshold scales primarily with action irreversibility.
 - Dynamic trust/intent scores do not safely survive cross-organization agent handoffs unless accompanied by inspectable underlying evidence, since a naked score invites blind reliance across context boundaries and can itself become a Contextual Integrity violation.
 - Treating a mid-workflow halt as a successful refusal can still strand users with irreversible losses (e.g., forfeited deposits), so compensating actions should be bound to the capability token (not just the planning layer) and paired with human-actionable summaries rather than forensic-only logs.

⚔️ **Hardening Agentic Web Populations Through Multi-Criterion Competitive Co-Evolution: Lessons from Wargame Strategy Optimization** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19319v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Shashank Raj, Kalyanmoy Deb (The University of Alabama)*

 The interesting twist is treating safety as a population-level arms race with multi-objective trade-offs, where the same machinery can both harden defenses and quantify the real cost of competition on each side.
 - Recasting jailbreaks and guardrails as two competing, co-evolving populations (with alternating update cycles and cross-play evaluation) targets the core failure mode of single-shot safety testing by continuously adapting both attack and defense rather than benchmarking a frozen snapshot.
 - Cross-play aggregation—scoring each candidate prompt/guardrail against the entire opposing population instead of a single opponent—directly reduces co-evolutionary overfitting and is positioned as the key mechanism for producing defenses that generalize to diverse, evolving jailbreak lineages.
 - A hypervolume-based “movement” gap (cooperative upper bound minus co-evolutionary equilibrium performance) is proposed as an operational safety diagnostic to quantify competitive pressure, i.e., how much guardrails measurably degrade attacker success and how much adaptive attacks measurably degrade defender usability.

🔗 **Agent-ZTA: A chain-Aware Zero Trust Model for Bidirectional Security-State Propagation and Runtime Containment in Multi-Agent Delegation** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18999v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Asadullah Abdullah Khan, Toqeer Ali Syed (Islamic University of Madinah)*

 The interesting part is the explicit linkage between authenticated delegation paths and runtime security-state changes—especially the reverse (downstream→upstream) workflow tainting plus a defined jail-mode containment option.
 - Agent-ZTA formalizes chain-aware continuous authorization by separating path-specific delegated authority (Dπ) from time- and context-dependent effective authority (Eπ(t,c)) and enforcing the invariant Eπ(t,c) ⊆ Dπ to prevent runtime state changes from ever expanding permissions beyond the original delegation bound.
 - Bidirectional security-state propagation is defined so that accepted upstream incidents can restrict descendants and accepted downstream incidents can taint and force reassessment of the relevant upstream workflow context, with policies designed to keep impacts branch-scoped so unrelated delegation paths remain unaffected.
 - The decision procedure is deterministic at the tool boundary (PEP/PDP) and explicitly fail-closed for missing or stale required security-state inputs by classifying them as “unavailable” rather than “normal,” trading some availability for stronger resistance to silent authorization under degraded observability.

🕶️ **SEOYUN: Refusal-Inducing Exam Documents for AI-Glasses Pipelines** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26033v1.pdf) #security `5/10` 

 *Dohyeon Kwon (PEACE-UNIVERSE)*

 A rare “reverse jailbreaking” paper that focuses on how to measure and bound answer leakage (not just refusal rates) in real exam-page capture pipelines, while explicitly disclaiming efficacy until held-out, physical, and human-legibility validation exists.
 - The framework formalizes an exam-specific threat model that separates detecting prohibited AI-glasses devices from reducing post-capture correct-answer leakage, explicitly warning that neither refusals nor RF signals should be used to attribute misconduct to individuals.
 - An illustrative sampling comparison shows that at d=350 mm and human acuity α=1 arcminute the geometric detail scale is ≈0.102 mm, while a pinhole camera with θ=100° and N=4032 yields ≈0.207 mm/pixel (and ≈0.543 mm/sample at 1536 px), implying that “make text tiny” is unlikely to create a reliable human-invisible but camera-readable channel under stated assumptions.
 - The protocol highlights key analytical limits for refusal-inducing documents—cropping or preprocessing can nullify policy-layer markers (so no universal L3 guarantee is possible), and removing an injected dummy answer choice does not change baseline guessing probability (1/q stays 1/q), requiring a matched benign dummy-option control to avoid false claims of efficacy.

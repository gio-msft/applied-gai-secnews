 A rare report-level STIX+ATT&CK ground-truth dataset that preserves entity relationships and shows local open-source LLMs can reliably audit annotations at scale.
 - A manually curated dataset structures 150 public English CTI reports (2015–2026) into STIX 2.1 report-level graphs totaling 4,777 entities and 5,817 directed relationships, averaging 32 nodes and 39 edges per report.
 - Adversarial behaviors are normalized via 1,273 STIX attack-pattern mappings to 269 unique MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise techniques/sub-techniques spanning all 15 tactics, enabling cross-report comparison despite vendor-specific terminology.
 - On a rigorously adjudicated 25-report reference subset (~17% of the corpus), human agreement was substantial (quadratic-weighted κ=0.64 type, 0.67 ATT&CK mapping, 0.62 relationships) and the best local LLM judge (Qwen3.6:27B) reached κ up to 0.803 with >92% micro-F1 and <5% false positives, indicating LLMs can flag inconsistencies but not replace expert validation.

🧾 **ReCon: A Resource-Constrained Benchmark for LLM-Based Cybersecurity Compliance Across Ingestion and Retrieval Pipelines** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22885v1.pdf) #cyber `7/10` 

 *Rohit Negi, Rishik Jain, Soumyo V Chakarborty et al. (Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur, International Institute of Information Technology Hyderabad)*

 It shows that practical, privacy-preserving compliance gap analysis can be done on commodity CPUs, but only if you pick the right small model and retrieval/prompting setup.
 - A CPU-only, on-prem RAG pipeline can automate ISO/IEC 27002:2022 policy documentary compliance checks at scale, covering 93 controls across 288 unique model+retrieval configurations for 26,784 control-level evaluations without requiring GPUs.
 - Among eight open-weight models, Granite4.1:8B achieved the strongest overall reliability (75.0% balanced accuracy, MCC 0.548, Cohen’s κ 0.528) and the highest mean agreement with auditor ground truth (80.65%), while several alternatives clustered below 60% balanced accuracy—indicating model choice dominates outcome quality under resource constraints.
 - Retrieval design materially shifts agreement, with the best-performing pipeline for Granite4.1:8B using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings plus ms-marco-MiniLM-L-12-v2 reranking (Case 2) reaching ~80.64% mean agreement, and prompt design can induce systematic errors (e.g., weaker models conflating standard requirement text with policy evidence and producing misleading confidence scores).

🔐 **Dynamic Capability Scoping for Enterprise AI Agents: A Synthetic Dataset and Three-Source Permission Architecture** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22445v1.pdf) #security `7/10` 

 *Halil Burak Noyan (Independent Researcher)*

 Dynamic least-privilege is treated as prevention (credentials absent) rather than detection (misuse noticed), with observe-only mode turning denied-permission attempts into a measurable misalignment signal.
 - A three-source permission architecture—role-based ceilings, a task-context permission classifier, and deterministic policy-derived combination prohibitions—aims to reduce enterprise agent attack surface by ensuring high-risk credentials never exist in context unless strictly required.
 - A released synthetic dataset of 600 enterprise task prompts labeled over a deployable 15-permission taxonomy achieved strong label reliability on a 10% human-reviewed sample, with Cohen’s κ improving from 0.917 to 0.967 after adjudication and exact-match rising from 68.3% to 85.0%.
 - Co-evolving synthetic prompt generation with the org policy exposed and fixed authorization gaps, cutting department ceiling violations from 46 to 3 (a 93% reduction), implying synthetic workloads can materially improve least-privilege IAM design before production rollout.

🤖 **Security of World-Model-Based Embodied AI: A Lifecycle of Threats, Defenses, and Evaluation** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28226v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Fazhong Liu, Zhuoyan Chen, Haozhen Tan et al.*

 The key insight is that predictive imagination and safety monitoring become attack surfaces themselves—so “looks safe in rollout” can be the failure mode.
 - World models create a new security boundary in embodied AI where compromises in data, sensors, prompts, memory, tools, or simulators can corrupt internal state and imagined rollouts, propagating into unsafe physical actions rather than merely incorrect predictions.
 - A lifecycle taxonomy clarifies that familiar attack classes (poisoning, backdoors, adversarial examples, sensor spoofing, prompt injection, trajectory manipulation, and supply-chain compromise) map onto distinct world-model security objects—especially state integrity, dynamics fidelity, trajectory-ranking integrity, uncertainty calibration, and feedback provenance—making trajectory-level and long-horizon failures first-class risks.
 - Using world models as runtime safety checkers is dual-use: they can block risky actions, but if subverted they can produce “predictive safety illusions” where an unsafe action is falsely certified as safe, implying evaluation must track predicted-safe-but-actually-unsafe executions and monitor confidence rather than only task success or collision rate.

🧪 **Cyber-Capable AI Agents: Vulnerabilities, Evaluation Containment, and Defensive Response** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25379v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Abu Bakar Siddik (Rajshahi University of Engineering & Technology)*

 The core takeaway is that cyber-capable agent evaluations must treat containment, tool privileges, and responder access as first-class security properties rather than background setup.
 - Indirect prompt-injection and memory-poisoning attacks can redirect tool-using agents at rates spanning ~24% to ~98% depending on scaffold and defenses, implying that “prompt safety” is an unreliable boundary once agents can retrieve, store, and act on untrusted content.
 - End-to-end exploit synthesis remains scaffold- and information-dependent (e.g., 157/898 ExploitGym instances solved in the strongest configuration, while one-day exploitation jumps from 7% to 87% when a CVE description is provided), so capability scores alone do not translate to real-world risk without explicit containment and privilege assumptions.
 - Defensive response is constrained by dual-use filtering: one benchmark reports a 2.72× higher refusal rate for defensive than neutral requests (with 43.8% refusals for system hardening and 34.3% for malware analysis), indicating that artifact-only safety gates can block incident responders unless role-aware access controls are engineered and evaluated.

🛡️ **TriShieldRAG: A Three-Ring Defense-in-Depth Framework Against Knowledge Corruption in Retrieval-Augmented Generation** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23838v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Susil Kumar Mohanty, Rohit Patel, Kosuru Yuvaraj et al. (Indian Institute of Technology Jodhpur)*

 The most interesting aspect is the explicit, formally stated failure boundary (minority-poison + provenance-tag assumptions) showing exactly when multi-stage RAG defenses should work and when they should be expected to fail.
 - A three-layer RAG defense (ingest-time lexical/statistical screening, retrieval-time provenance+consistency trust re-ranking, and generation-time cross-model voting with one bounded re-retrieval) reduced knowledge-poisoning attack success from ~91% to ~13% on a 5,000-document Wikipedia KB with 10 targeted questions while preserving benign-query accuracy.
 - Layered defenses hinge on explicit preconditions: retrieval/generation aggregation is only expected to work when poisoned documents remain a minority of the retrieved top‑k (ρ<0.5), because once poison becomes the majority it can dominate both inter-document “consistency” signals and majority voting, yielding confident but wrong answers.
 - Provenance weighting can provide a hard safety floor for correctly tagged trusted sources (a clean document’s trust score is guaranteed ≥0.45, above the drop threshold ϑ2=0.35), but this protection collapses if attackers can spoof or obtain trusted provenance metadata, making provenance attestation and write-access controls a high-leverage deployment requirement.

🗂️ **Share No More Than the Request Requires: Federated Disclosure for Perspective-Aware AI** [source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22953v1.pdf) #security `6/10` 

 *Sourena Khanzadeh, Daniel Platnick, Marjan Alirezaie et al. (Toronto Metropolitan University, Flybits Labs, MIT Media Lab)*

 It turns selective disclosure into a graph-minimization problem with provenance-first responses, aiming to operationalize “share no more than the request requires” across federated, agent-mediated identity queries.
 - Provenance Preserving Chronicles (PPC) enforces minimum-necessary disclosure by compiling each holder’s temporal, predicate-labeled identity graph into a compact, relationship- and purpose-authorized evidence subgraph, so peers can be queried without centralizing raw personal data.
 - The framework separates authorization from sufficiency by (i) evaluating a local authorization function over every node/edge and (ii) validating a candidate release via structural requirements—ontology-typed path coverage, predicate-type completeness, connectivity, and provenance completeness—before anything is returned to the requester.
 - A two-phase “text-first, artifacts-second” release flow reduces irreversible high-fidelity leakage by returning provenance-linked textual answers initially and requiring explicit holder approval for any raw artifact access, while explicitly modeling post-disclosure risks like downstream inference and prompt injection as remaining hazards.
